# Growing as an Architect: Katas and Practice

## Why deliberate practice

Becoming an architect takes far more than picking a frontend library or sketching boxes on a whiteboard. It combines seeing the whole system, ranking quality attributes, making trade-off after trade-off, and handling organizational politics while communicating upward, downward and sideways. Skill comes from designing many systems, yet most architects design only a handful in a whole career. Ted Neward's architectural katas address that gap: short problem statements across many domains with typical constraints and deliberately incomplete detail, leaving room for interpretation.

## Solo or small-group kata session (1 to 2 hours)

1. Pick a kata (or draw one at random).
2. List the questions you would need answered before designing; write down assumptions for each.
3. Extract and rank the quality attributes; note the ones that are implied but unstated.
4. Name the riskiest or most complex part, and what worries you most.
5. Recommend a style and justify it against the ranked attributes; note the runner-up and why it lost.
6. Write one or two ADRs and at least two fitness functions for the top attributes.
7. Sketch one diagram for a chosen audience.

## Revisit and review

- Shelve the solution for days or weeks, then reread it: what would you change, what did you miss, would you now pick another approach?
- Run an informal review with colleagues: collect their questions and identify gaps.
- Keep a cadence: a new kata, or a deeper pass on an old one, every month or two.
- For a tougher test, form a team and join a live Architectural Katas event (O'Reilly runs them).

## Katas as an interview tool

Instead of brainteasers or trivia, walk a candidate through a kata. The questions they ask reveal how they think, and the collaborative back-and-forth previews what working together would be like. Evaluate: do they ask about users, load, data and constraints before naming technologies? Do they state trade-offs? Do they adapt when you add a constraint?

## How an agent can coach with katas

- Act as the "customer": answer clarifying questions with deliberately uneven detail, and add a late constraint (new region, budget cut, regulation) to test adaptability.
- Grade the learner's answer on: questions asked, attribute ranking, explicit trade-offs, style rationale, ADR quality, fitness functions, audience-appropriate diagram.
- Invented kata prompts for practice: a library-book reservation app for a city network; a farm sensor platform with intermittent connectivity; a ticket marketplace for regional football clubs; an internal document search for a 3,000-person bank.
