# Career Management (FSE Chapter 14)

## Purpose

Help a developer own their career deliberately instead of drifting. The chapter splits the job in two: planning (find what energizes you, see the full menu of roles, work backward from a 3 to 5 year target, learn on purpose, weigh offers against your life phase) and walking the path (log wins, reframe self-doubt, cultivate relationships, interview well, survive rejection, protect your health and boundaries, including when remote). Apply these rules when coaching, reviewing a plan, or drafting career artifacts with the user.

## Key decision rules

1. **Interests are discovered, not deduced.** If the user does not know what excites them, prescribe experiments (different domains, side projects in an unfamiliar stack) plus an energy journal scoring activities +1/-1 with a reason. Look for patterns after a few weeks.
2. **Calibrate side-project tech against the market.** Sample postings from about 10 target employers and count recurring technologies; then deliberately add at least one thing unlike the day job.
3. **Almost nothing is irreversible.** Only ethical violations and burned bridges are effectively permanent; stack, employer and even track can be changed. Use this to lower decision anxiety.
4. **Three families of paths exist:** technical expert (architect, staff/principal), leadership (engineering manager, technical product manager), and hybrid/alternative (developer advocate, sales engineer, entrepreneur, SRE, DevOps). Senior IC pay can match management, so management is not the only way up.
5. **Self-advocacy is required.** Tell the manager the goal; ask for shadowing or rotations; invite people in the target role to coffee and ask about their journey.
6. **Plan backward.** Pick a 3 to 5 year destination, then define milestones for years 1-2, 3-5 and 5+. Each milestone must be observable (led X, mentored Y), and should include non-technical skills.
7. **Random learning yields random results.** Fundamentals first (algorithms, data structures, design patterns, testing, version control, database design), then a T shape: one deep stack plus adjacent breadth. Reject resume-driven development.
8. **Filter every learning opportunity** on five questions: goal alignment, relevance in 3 to 5 years, competitive edge, value to current team, personal excitement. Excitement counts; without it time and attention will not follow.
9. **Keep a personal tech radar** (4 quadrants x Adopt/Trial/Assess/Hold) and review it quarterly alongside the roadmap.
10. **Weigh jobs on five factors** (pay, culture, work content, growth/opportunity cost, work-life balance) and reweight by life phase: early career favors growth, family formation favors stability and benefits, mid-career favors compensation moves, later career favors balance, culture and legacy.
11. **Log wins specifically and with dates.** Metric + action + outcome beats vague claims. The log feeds reviews, resumes, interviews and imposter-syndrome relief.
12. **Treat imposter feelings as a signal of care and of awareness of the unknown**, not evidence of inadequacy. Be wary of anyone who claims to know everything. Counter with action: repeat exposure, welcome review feedback, pick unfamiliar tickets.
13. **Networking means relationships, not follower counts.** Principles: authenticity, dependability, mutual value, respect, clear communication. Contact people when you need nothing; schedule recurring check-ins.
14. **Interview as a two-way evaluation.** Research before applying and again after landing the interview; learn the interview style; prepare questions; never badmouth an employer; think aloud before coding; request feedback after rejection; never burn bridges.
15. **AI is a research and rehearsal aid, not a ghostwriter.** Use it for company research and voice-mode mock interviews, validate its output, and do not submit unedited AI cover letters or resumes.
16. **Name the stressors and pair each with a countermeasure**: tech churn (radar, deliberate learning, peers), problems that will not switch off (step away, rubber duck), distributed teams (clear channels, shared tooling), health (boundaries, self-care). Remote work needs explicit fixes for isolation, mentorship access, outages and visibility.

## Default workflow

1. Clarify the user's current role, life phase, constraints and what they enjoy or dread.
2. If direction is unclear, set up experiments and an energy journal before planning.
3. Choose a target role and draft a backward milestone plan.
4. Build or update the tech radar; run candidate skills through the five-question filter.
5. For a job decision, score options on the five factors with phase-based weights and the practical checklist.
6. Set up the habits: wins log, relationship check-ins, quarterly radar review.
7. For an upcoming interview, run the prep, rehearsal, day-of and follow-up playbook.
8. Check sustainability: boundaries, remote-work risks, burnout signs.
